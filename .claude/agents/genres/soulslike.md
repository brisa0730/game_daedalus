# Soulslike

## Core Fun Elements
- Pattern learning: Reading, memorizing, and mastering enemy movesets
- Overcoming triumph: The rush of defeating what seemed impossible
- Fair difficulty: Deaths always feel like the player's fault, not the game's

## Gold Standard
- Elden Ring: Open world that respects Souls difficulty — freedom to leave and return
- Sekiro: Rhythm-based combat that redefines "parry" as a core mechanic
- Hollow Knight: 2D proof that Soulslike design transcends 3D

## Player Expectations
- High but fair difficulty
- Readable enemy patterns learnable through repetition
- Death-as-learning loop (every death teaches something)
- Exploration-found lore and shortcuts
- Weighty, committed combat (no animation canceling)
- Risk/reward soul-like resource mechanics
- Interconnected 3D level design

## Player Demands
- Every enemy is learnable-to-victory guarantee
- Consistent hit detection
- Appropriate checkpoint placement
- Boss challenge that feels earned, not cheap

## Delight Points
- No-hit clearing a boss after 50 deaths
- Discovering a shortcut that connects two distant areas
- Lore connections clicking into place
- Feeling genuine skill growth over time

## Genre Landmines
- Unfair/inconsistent hit detection
- Long boss runback corridors
- Frustrating rather than hard (unfair enemy tracking, camera issues)
- Critical information hidden too deeply
- Stat-scaling that trivializes challenge

## Anti-patterns
- False Soulslikes (hard but without learning curve)
- Randomized untouchable AI
- Mandatory healing item farming
- Barren exploration with no reward
- "Harder" bosses via stat inflation only
