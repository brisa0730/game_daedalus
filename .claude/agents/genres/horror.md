# Horror

## Core Fun Elements
- Anxiety management: Sustained unease that the player must cope with
- Information scarcity: Not knowing what's ahead is scarier than seeing it
- Relief rhythm: Tension needs release to be effective

## Gold Standard
- RE series (RE2 Remake): Resource management meets spatial horror
- Silent Hill 2: Psychological horror through environmental storytelling
- Amnesia: Helplessness as the core mechanic

## Player Expectations
- Progressive unease (building dread, not constant jump scares)
- Resource scarcity
- Threatening encounters
- Environmental storytelling
- Immersive sound design
- Safe zones for relief

## Player Demands
- Fear pacing (tension → climax → relief cycles)
- Multi-layered fear sources (not one-note)
- Player agency (flee/hide/fight options)
- Darkness integrated into gameplay (not just aesthetics)

## Delight Points
- First enemy encounter shock
- Story reveals that recontextualize earlier fear
- Safe room relief after intense sections
- Overcoming fear through courage and skill

## Genre Landmines
- Jump-scare dependency (cheap thrills)
- Late-game combat focus (horror becomes action)
- Overly scripted horror (predictable scares)
- Maze frustration (lost, not scared)
- Excessive darkness (can't see anything, not fun)

## Anti-patterns
- Jump-scare machines (quantity over quality)
- Too-frequent enemies (desensitization)
- Purposeless horror (scary but no meaning)
- Missing safe zones (no relief = player exhaustion)
- Excessive failure states (frustration replaces fear)
